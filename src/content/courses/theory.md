Theory of Computation offers a formal view of what problems computers can represent and solve.

- Simple computational models can clarify difficult questions.
- Definitions carry much of the reasoning.
- Some limits belong to the problem, not the implementation.

## My experience so far

The course rewards precision and makes intuitive claims earn their justification. Automata, languages, and proofs provide a different perspective from building software, but strengthen the same habit of careful reasoning.

# What stayed with me

Formal models are useful because they remove distractions. By limiting what a machine can do, they make the essential difficulty of a problem easier to see.

## Advice for someone taking it

1. Rewrite definitions in your own words before using them.
2. Draw automata and work through short strings by hand.
3. Practice proofs regularly rather than only before an assessment.
