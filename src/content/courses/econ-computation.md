Economics and Computation sits directly between my two majors: algorithms meet incentives, strategic behavior, and market design.

- Algorithms change the behavior of participants.
- Efficiency and fairness may suggest different choices.
- Mechanism design begins with explicit assumptions.

# My experience so far

The course is helping me examine technical systems as environments where people respond to incentives rather than as neutral collections of features.

# What stayed with me

A system can be computationally impressive and still produce the wrong behavior. Before designing the mechanism, it helps to name the actors, what each person knows, and what each person wants.

## Advice for someone taking it

1. Review algorithmic reasoning and basic microeconomics.
2. State the actors, information, and incentives before solving the model.
3. Question whether a mathematically clean outcome fits the real setting.
